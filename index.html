What your site is costing you — Leading Edge Performance

I'm Jarrett, a web designer here in Las Vegas. I went through your site the way someone looking for a performance shop would, on a phone. Here's what I saw.

The short version: someone searching for a Camaro, Corvette, CTS-V or truck shop in Vegas finds you, but your homepage gives them very little to go on before they decide whether to call.

1. The homepage doesn't show what you do. Below the headline there's a newsletter signup, a short contact form and your address. It doesn't list your services, show your builds, or give any reason to pick you over the next shop. That's about 200 words on the whole page. People who can't quickly see "they do exactly what I need" keep scrolling through search results.

2. There's no way to book. You don't have a "Schedule Service" or "Get a Quote" button anywhere on the homepage. The only options are a small form asking for a name and email, or calling during weekday hours. Someone browsing at night or on a Saturday, when you're closed, has no clear next step.

3. Your car photos are invisible to Google. None of the 24 images on the homepage have a description. Google can't tell they show your builds, and people using screen readers hear nothing. The page is also slow to settle on a phone: in my phone test the main content took 6.8 seconds to appear.

4. Small things that hurt trust: "Nevada" is misspelled "Neveda" in your Google description, the headline reads "ShOP", the footer says © 2018, and one file the page asks for is missing.

What's working: your phone number is tappable, and your address, hours and directions are right on the homepage.

What we couldn't measure: how many people visit, where you rank, and whether contact-form messages reach you. I didn't submit anything, so that's unmeasured.
